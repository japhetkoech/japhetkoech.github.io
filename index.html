About

I work on the stability of grids that run on inverters.

I'm a power systems engineer (MSc) focused on the small-signal and electromagnetic-transient behavior of grid-following and grid-forming converters, and the oscillation dynamics that decide whether a low-inertia system stays stable.

My hands-on work runs from modal analysis of multi-machine networks — eigenvalues, participation factors, short-circuit ratio — to detailed converter and machine modeling in MATLAB/Simulink and Python. My research trajectory centers on airborne wind energy grid integration: hybrid battery–supercapacitor storage for pulsating sources today, and grid-forming control for time-periodic systems (LTP / Floquet) on the longer horizon.

Alongside the research I'm working toward NERC Reliability Coordinator certification and building practical understanding of power systems operations and grid reliability compliance, and I care about making this material teachable — most of the writing below started as an attempt to explain it clearly to myself.

Selected projects

01WSCC 9-Bus Small-Signal Stability Model

A full modal analysis of the WSCC 9-bus system with mixed synchronous and inverter-based generation — a replication-and-extension of the NREL study on IBR small-signal behavior. The network is linearized, the state-space A-matrix assembled, and its eigenvalues read mode-by-mode; participation factors trace every critical mode to the machines that drive it, and SCR / WSCR / gSCR quantify how grid strength shifts with inverter penetration. Built on the G2ELin toolbox over EMT Simulink models.

02Grid-Following (GFL) Inverter Modeling

A detailed grid-following converter around the SRF-PLL, dq current control, and the LCL filter — the dominant fleet architecture, and the one whose hidden synchronization assumption breaks as the grid weakens. The model exposes where PLL bandwidth and grid impedance interact to create the negative-resistance behavior behind sub-synchronous control interactions; small-signal impedance is extracted for impedance-based Nyquist assessment.

03Grid-Forming (GFM) VSM Control

A grid-forming converter acting as a controllable voltage source behind an impedance — imposing frequency and voltage rather than tracking them — with virtual synchronous machine emulation so it delivers inertia and damping through a synthetic swing equation. The study compares droop, VSM, dVOC, and matching control on tracking, transient response, and stability margin as short-circuit ratio falls into the weak-grid regime.

04Detailed Synchronous Generator Modeling

A full dq0 synchronous machine in the rotor frame — stator and rotor flux-linkage equations, damper windings, and electromechanical swing dynamics — the physical reference against which every inverter control is judged. The derivation works through the Park transformation rigorously, including the speed-voltage cross-coupling terms it introduces. This is what "synthetic inertia" from a grid-forming inverter is trying to reproduce.

05Short-Circuit & SCR Analysis

Grid-strength characterization at the interconnection point: fault-current computation and the family of ratios — SCR, weighted, composite, and gSCR — that set how much inverter capacity a bus can host before margins collapse, each tied to a concrete interconnection outcome and back to the modal results in 01.

06Three-Phase Induction Machine Theory

An arbitrary-reference-frame qd0 induction machine, built block-by-block from the Krause formulation in MATLAB/Simulink — voltage, flux-linkage, torque, and mechanical equations assembled programmatically through the Simulink API — developing the cross-coupling speed-voltage terms the transformation produces, with the arbitrary frame as the general case.

07Hybrid Battery–Supercapacitor Sizing for Airborne Wind Energy

Thesis work: frequency-domain optimal sizing of a hybrid battery–supercapacitor store that smooths the pulsating power of an airborne wind system. EIS characterizes each device; a Butterworth power split routes high-frequency content to the supercapacitor and low-frequency to the battery; split point and capacities are chosen by convex optimization in CVXPY under a flicker-compliance constraint.

08LTP / HSS Modeling with Floquet Stability

Converters driven by a periodically varying source are linear time-periodic, not LTI. This builds the harmonic state-space representation that captures the harmonic coupling an LTI model misses, and applies Floquet theory to judge stability of the periodic orbit — the analytical backbone of grid-forming control for pulsating sources, where mirror-frequency coupling drives the dominant modes.
